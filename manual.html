nano-ToonTalk

A workshop where you teach little robots by showing them what to do — and everything, even a whole workshop, is a thing you can pick up.

The basics

You do everything with single clicks: click a thing to pick it up, and it follows your pointer; click where you want it to set it down. Drag with the mouse button held to orbit the camera; scroll to zoom. The row of dark pedestals in a semicircle behind the robot's work area are stacks — endless supplies of numbers, text pads, boxes, scales, nests, rooms and little robots. Click a stack to take the top one.

The wooden table in front is your workbench. Robots can never reach it — that is the point: what you build there is yours until you hand it over.

Everything in the manual's little windows below is the real workshop — click things, carry them, try it.

Numbers

Numbers come off their stack as 1s. While holding one, type digits to set its value, - to make it negative, and * / ^ to choose what it does when dropped on another number (adding is the default — and adding a negative is subtraction). Dropping a number on a number combines them: the arriving one applies its operation. Division gives exact fractions, never rounded decimals.

A number block shows its value on the front and top; walk the camera close and the other faces show the same value in English words, scientific notation, and decimal form — with a bar over a repeating part when it fits.

Text pads

Text pads are paper for words. Type on a held pad to write (Enter for a new line, Ctrl+V pastes). Drop one pad on the left edge of another and it reads first; on the right edge and it reads last — "Talk" dropped on the right of "Toon" makes "ToonTalk". A whole number dropped on a written pad shifts its first or last letter along the alphabet; on a blank pad, the number writes itself out as digits.

Try it: pick up "Talk" and drop it on the right edge of "Toon". Then try the 2 on a pad's left edge.

Boxes and scales

Boxes have holes, and holes hold anything — numbers, pads, nests, other boxes, even little robots (they lie down). Type a digit while holding a box to give it that many holes. Boxes are arrays: drop a box on a box to join them — left half to put its holes in front, right half behind — and shrinking a box splits the extra holes off as a box of their own.

Scales have two pans that work exactly like holes; the beam tips toward the larger number, so a robot's eye can compare.

Try it: put the 8 and 9 in the holes. Hold the box and type 4. Take a second box from the stack and join them.

Birds and nests

Take a nest from its stack and set it down: the egg hatches into a bird — they are a pair for life. Give the bird anything and she flies it home to her nest, tucking new deliveries under the pile so the top is always the oldest. Click the top of a pile to take one delivery; grab the tower lower down and you pick up the whole nest, deliveries and all.

Copy a bird on the copier and both birds serve the same nest. Copy a nest and you have a broadcast: give any of their birds something and every copy of the nest receives its own copy at once.

Try it: wait for the egg to hatch, then drop the 3 on the bird and watch the delivery. Give her the 7 next — it goes under.

Robots

Take a mini robot from its stack, set it on the table, and drop something on it: it grows, steps behind the bench, and its thought bubble opens — you are now teaching it. Everything you make it do (take things, drop them, fetch from stacks, use the copier) is remembered as steps about its own things — "hole 1 of what it was given", never places in your world. Leave the bubble and the daydream rewinds; the robot keeps the lesson.

Its thought bubble shows the condition: the shape it must be given before it will run. Give a trained robot something that fits and it gets to work at once, repeating while the condition still matches, up to the Rounds limit. Drop one trained robot on another to form a team.

Click the bubble of a robot (big or mini) to take out a copy of the very thing it was first shown. Type while holding a robot to name it — the name appears on its chest. To save a robot, hold it and press Save held thing.

Try it: drop the 5 on the mini robot. In the thought bubble, click the number stack, then click a work spot; leave the bubble and it will run. Then erase its thought with Ruby and give it anything.

Dusty and Ruby

Two helpers live in front of the table. Dusty the vacuum removes: click him to wake him, then click things to vacuum them away, entries off notebook pages, or parts of a robot's thought (leaving an empty hole, which matches anything or nothing). Ruby the eraser generalises: wake her and click a part of a thought — a 7 becomes any number, then anything. She also erases real things in place: an erased number stands for any number, which is how you build a robot's expectations by hand. Waking one settles the other, and whoever is awake fidgets.

Rooms

Rooms are little houses that each hold an entire workshop. Drop a robot on a room and it steps inside, ready behind the desk. Drop anything else on the room and it lands on that desk — and the robot inside gets to work the moment your world goes quiet. The chimney smokes while there is work going on.

Click the roof to switch between solid walls (the room works instantly, unseen) and glass (you watch it work, round by round, at your speed setting). If its robot stops at the Rounds limit, click the roof to set it going again. Wake Dusty and click a working room to interrupt it. Click the door to step inside: the room becomes the world around you, and the world you left shrinks onto the table as a little room of its own — walk back out through its door. Birds fly in and out of rooms to reach their nests, so a machine in a box can feed a nest on your table.

Try it: drop the mini robot on the room, then the 4. Click the roof to make the walls glass. Click the door to step inside.

The notebook, saving and sharing

The notebook on your table is a two-page spread. Drop anything on a blank page to file it; click a filed entry to take out a copy (the page keeps its own). Drop a written text pad on a full page to open the first page mentioning that text; a whole number jumps to that page number. Only Dusty can remove an entry, and a notebook itself only goes when open at two blank pages. The first page holds a blank notebook — copy it out to make more. The main notebook remembers everything between visits.

Save held thing writes whatever is in your hand to a file — a box, a trained robot, a room with a whole workshop inside. Save world writes everything. Import file (or dropping a file onto the app) brings either back: things land in your hand, worlds replace the workshop.

nano-ToonTalk is an open experiment in rebuilding ToonTalk's ideas with real-time 3D — source on GitHub.
